# "The Island That Was Already Empty": original script v1 (2026-11-02)
Format: ~60 s vertical short, ~3.7 words/s, one steady narrator voice. Our own story and wording.

## Facts (with sources)
- Japan occupied Kiska, Aleutian Islands, Alaska, from June 1942. It captured the US Navy weather station there and
  its pet dog, named Explosion. (Wikipedia: Japanese occupation of Kiska; Kiska)
- 28 July 1943: Japanese ships slipped into Kiska Harbor under fog and took off the whole garrison, about 5,183 men,
  in roughly 50 minutes, undetected. (NPS "Invasion of Kiska"; Pacific Wrecks)
- Allied planes kept bombing the island in the weeks before the landing. (NPS; Wikipedia: Operation Cottage)
- 15 August 1943: about 35,000 US and Canadian troops landed (Operation Cottage). (World War II Database; Wikipedia)
- In the fog, US and Canadian troops fired on each other: 28 Americans and 4 Canadians killed (Wikipedia: Kiska).
  Plus booby traps and mines. (NPS: over 100 friendly-fire casualties.)
- 18 August 1943: the destroyer USS Abner Read hit a mine off Kiska, about 70 killed. (Wikipedia: USS Abner Read)
- Total Allied casualties: over 300 (313 per project44.ca; Task & Purpose: 92 dead, 221 wounded).
- The troops found no Japanese, only a few dogs, one of them Explosion. (The Clio; Wikipedia: Kiska)

## Script (224 words, about 61 s at 3.7 words/s)
Thirty-five thousand soldiers stormed an island, lost over three hundred men, and never found a single enemy.
It's 1943, and Japan has been holding Kiska, a freezing rock off the coast of Alaska, for over a year.
One foggy afternoon, Japanese ships sneak into the harbor and load up every last soldier in under an hour.
Nobody notices.
For the next two weeks, Allied planes keep bombing the empty island anyway.
Then thirty-five thousand American and Canadian troops storm the beaches, ready for the fight of their lives.
The fog is so thick they can't see their own boots.
Somebody hears a noise and opens fire. Somebody else fires back.
All night, two armies trade shots in the dark, and both of them are on the same side.
Luckily, the enemy wasn't there.
Unluckily, everybody else was.
You see, when your eyes can't see, your brain fills in the blanks.
Ever walked into a dark room and thought the coat on the door was a person?
Now give everyone in that room a rifle.
Between the fog, the booby traps, and a navy ship that hit a mine, over three hundred men were killed or wounded.
And when they finally searched the whole island, the only ones left to surrender were a few dogs.
And one of them, named Explosion, used to be American.
