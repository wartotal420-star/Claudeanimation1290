## 0. COLD OPEN

*Black screen. A single domino with a tiny stick soldier on it. It tips. Fast montage: tanks, planes, ships, a mushroom cloud far away, then a lone soldier in a jungle, still holding his rifle.*

In 1939, a war started that would pull in almost every country on Earth.
It would kill more than seventy million people, flatten whole cities, and end with a weapon so powerful that nobody has dared use it in a war since.
And one soldier would keep fighting it until 1974. [beat]
This is the entire history of World War Two.
How it started, how it almost went the other way, and how it finally ended. [beat]
And to understand it, we have to start with the war before it.

## 1. THE BAD PEACE (1919–1933) [CHAPTER]

*Paris, 1919. A long table. Stick leaders in top hats. A tiny German stick figure in the corner, sweating.*

It's 1919. World War One is finally over, and the winners meet in Paris to decide what happens to Germany.
Spoiler: nothing good.
Germany has to admit the war was its fault, give up land, shrink its army to just one hundred thousand men, and pay for the damage.
The bill is so big that Germany is still technically paying it off in 2010. [beat]

*A German stick figure pushing a wheelbarrow full of cash. A loaf of bread with a giant price tag.*

The money runs out fast.
To pay its bills, the German government does the obvious thing.
It prints more money.
Then more. Then a lot more.
By 1923, a single loaf of bread costs two hundred billion marks.
People are paid twice a day so they can shop before prices go up again.
Kids use stacks of cash as building blocks, because it's cheaper than buying toys. [beat]

*Calm for a moment: lights in windows, jazz. Then a stock market chart falls off a cliff.*

Things calm down for a few years.
Then, in 1929, the American stock market crashes, and the whole world goes broke with it.
In Germany, six million people lose their jobs.
And when people are hungry, angry, and scared, they start listening to anyone who sounds sure of himself. [beat]

*A small, intense stick man with a tiny mustache on a beer crate, yelling. A crowd slowly grows.*

Enter Adolf Hitler.
A failed art student who was rejected by a Vienna art school twice.
He served in World War One, came home furious, and joined a tiny political party that met in the back room of a beer hall.
Within a few years, he's running it.
He tells Germans exactly what they want to hear: none of this was your fault, it was someone else's, and I'll make Germany powerful again.
In January 1933, he becomes Chancellor of Germany.
Within months, every other party is banned, newspapers are controlled, and the people he blamed are being pushed out of public life. [beat]
Germany isn't a democracy anymore.
It's one man's country.

## 2. EVERYONE GRABS SOMETHING (1931–1938) [CHAPTER]

*World map as a tabletop. Three stick leaders reach for pieces at once: Germany, Italy, Japan.*

Here's the thing. Hitler isn't the only one who wants more.
Over in Asia, Japan is an island nation with big ambitions and almost no oil, iron, or rubber of its own.
So in 1931, Japanese officers blow up a small piece of their own railway in Manchuria, in northern China, blame the Chinese, and use it as an excuse to take the whole region. [beat]

*A stick diplomat at a big round table (League of Nations) wags a finger. The Japanese stick diplomat stands up and walks out.*

The League of Nations, which was basically the world's group chat for keeping the peace, is outraged.
It writes a very strongly worded report.
Japan reads it, and leaves the group chat. [beat]
In 1937, Japan invades the rest of China.
Japan's generals expect it to be over in a few months.
It lasts eight years. [beat]

*Tone drops. A city wall at dusk. Smoke. No violence shown, just empty streets and a single dropped shoe.*

In December 1937, Japanese troops capture China's capital, Nanjing.
For six weeks, soldiers murder, rape, and loot their way through the city.
Tens of thousands of people are killed, and some historians believe it was hundreds of thousands. [beat]
The Chinese government retreats deep into the country and keeps fighting.
And that matters more than most people realise.
For the rest of the war, around a million Japanese soldiers are stuck in China, soldiers Japan will badly need everywhere else.

*Italy: a stick man with a big chin and an even bigger balcony. Mussolini.*

Meanwhile in Italy, a dictator named Benito Mussolini dreams of rebuilding the Roman Empire.
So in 1935, he invades Ethiopia, one of the last independent countries in Africa.
The League of Nations is outraged again.
And again, it does basically nothing. [beat]

*Hitler watching both events on a tiny TV, taking notes.*

Hitler is watching all of this and learning one lesson.
Nobody is going to stop him.
In 1936, he marches troops into the Rhineland, a strip of Germany that was supposed to stay empty of soldiers.
His generals are terrified. They have orders to turn around if the French army fights back.
The French army does not fight back. [beat]

*Map pieces sliding toward Germany: Austria, then the Sudetenland.*

In 1938, Germany swallows Austria without firing a shot.
Then Hitler demands a piece of Czechoslovakia called the Sudetenland.
Britain and France really, really don't want another war.
So their leaders fly to Munich and make a deal: Hitler gets the Sudetenland, and in return, he promises that's all he wants. [beat]

*British stick PM stepping off a plane, waving a piece of paper. Crowd cheering.*

The British Prime Minister, Neville Chamberlain, flies home waving the agreement and tells everyone he's secured peace for our time.
Six months later, Hitler takes the rest of Czechoslovakia anyway.
Peace for our time lasted about as long as a gym membership in January.

## 3. THE DEAL AND THE INVASION (1939) [CHAPTER]

*Two stick men across a table: Hitler and Stalin. They hate each other. They shake hands anyway, sweating.*

Now, there's one man Hitler hates even more than the British and the French.
Joseph Stalin, the dictator of the Soviet Union.
Hitler wrote a whole book about wanting to take land in the East. Stalin's land.
So in August 1939, the world is stunned when Germany and the Soviet Union sign a deal promising not to attack each other. [beat]
But the deal has a secret part.
They've already agreed to split Poland between them, like two kids dividing a pizza that isn't theirs.

*Night. A Polish border post. Then a tank rolls through the barrier.*

On the first of September 1939, Germany invades Poland.
Two days later, Britain and France keep their promise to Poland and declare war on Germany.
World War Two has officially begun. [beat]

*Polish cavalry on horses. A freeze frame with a big "MYTH" stamp.*

You might have heard that Polish cavalry charged German tanks with swords.
That's a myth.
Polish riders charged a group of German infantry, won, and then got caught by armored cars that showed up afterwards.
German propaganda turned it into a story about brave idiots charging tanks, and it stuck for eighty years. [beat]

*Second front: Soviet tanks rolling in from the east. Poland squeezed between two walls.*

Poland fights hard.
But on the seventeenth of September, the Soviet Union invades from the other side.
Squeezed from both directions, Poland falls in about five weeks.
And Britain and France, who just declared war to save it... do almost nothing.

## 4. THE FAKE WAR AND THE FAST WAR (1939–1940) [CHAPTER]

*French stick soldiers playing cards in a bunker. A British bomber drops... paper.*

For the next seven months, the war in Western Europe is weirdly quiet.
The French sit behind their giant line of fortresses called the Maginot Line.
British bombers fly over Germany and drop... leaflets.
Millions of paper leaflets, politely asking the Germans to please stop.
The British start calling it the Bore War. [beat]

*Snowy forest. Finnish ski troops in white, gliding past a long, stuck Soviet column.*

The real fighting is happening way up north.
Stalin attacks Finland, expecting an easy win.
Instead, Finnish soldiers on skis, dressed in white, ambush his frozen columns in the forests.
The Soviets lose so many men that the whole world realises the Red Army might not be as strong as it looks.
Hitler takes notes again. [beat]

*Fast montage: Denmark map, a clock showing six hours. Norway fjords, ships.*

In April 1940, Germany invades Denmark, which surrenders in about six hours.
Then Norway, which takes two months.
And then, on the tenth of May 1940, Germany finally turns west. [beat]

*The Maginot Line from above: huge, impressive. Camera pans north to a thick forest, where German tanks quietly drive between the trees.*

France has the bigger army and more tanks.
It also has the Maginot Line, the most expensive wall in Europe.
So Germany simply drives around it.
German tanks squeeze through the Ardennes, a thick forest the French thought was too dense for tanks.
It wasn't. [beat]
Here's the trick that makes this so fast.
Instead of moving slowly with the infantry like in the last war, German tanks punch through one weak spot and keep going, with planes clearing the road ahead.
It's like a whole army skipping the queue. [beat]

*Beach. Thousands of tiny stick soldiers in lines. A fleet of small boats, fishing boats, yachts, a paddle steamer.*

Within days, the British army and a big chunk of the French are trapped against the sea at a town called Dunkirk.
They should be finished.
But Hitler orders his tanks to stop for a few days, and Britain grabs the chance.
Navy ships, fishing boats, ferries, and even private yachts sail across the Channel to pick them up.
In nine days, they rescue more than three hundred thousand soldiers. [beat]

*A railway carriage in a forest. A German stick officer smugly holding the door open.*

France isn't so lucky.
Paris falls, and in June 1940 France surrenders.
Hitler makes them sign it in the exact same railway carriage where Germany surrendered in 1918.
He even had it pulled out of a museum for the occasion.
That's not a peace deal. That's a grudge. [beat]
In six weeks, Germany has done what it couldn't do in four years last time.
And now, Britain is alone.

## 5. BRITAIN ALONE (1940–1941) [CHAPTER]

*A stocky stick man with a cigar and a bowler hat: Churchill. Behind him, a wall map of Europe that's almost all one color.*

Britain has a new Prime Minister, Winston Churchill.
And Churchill has one very simple plan: never give up.
Hitler expects Britain to make peace.
Britain does not make peace. [beat]

*Sky over England. Tiny fighter planes chasing each other. A stick woman at a big radar screen, pointing.*

So Hitler decides to invade.
But first, he needs to control the sky.
In the summer of 1940, the German air force throws wave after wave of planes at Britain.
What they don't know is that Britain has a secret weapon: radar.
A chain of towers along the coast can see German planes coming before they even reach the shore.
So British fighters are always waiting for them. [beat]
The Germans lose so many planes that Hitler quietly cancels the invasion.
It's the first time anybody has really stopped him.

*Night. London. Searchlights. Families sleeping on an Underground platform.*

Instead, he bombs British cities at night.
For eight months, Londoners sleep in underground train stations, then climb out every morning and go to work anyway.
That's the Blitz. [beat]

*Africa: Italian stick soldiers marching forward confidently. Cut to: the same soldiers walking backwards with their hands up, in a very long line.*

Meanwhile, Mussolini wants a win of his own.
He invades British-held Egypt with a huge army.
The British counterattack with a much smaller one, and capture over a hundred thousand Italian soldiers.
Then he invades Greece. The Greeks push him back too. [beat]

*Hitler facepalming. A stick general in goggles waves from a tank in the desert: Rommel.*

So Hitler has to keep rescuing his ally.
He sends troops into Greece and Yugoslavia, and sends a general named Erwin Rommel to North Africa.
Rommel is so good at surprise attacks in the desert that the British start calling him the Desert Fox. [beat]

*An American stick president in a wheelchair at a desk, signing a paper. Crates labelled "TANKS", "FOOD", "PLANES" floating across an ocean to Britain.*

Across the ocean, America is officially staying out of the war.
But President Franklin Roosevelt starts sending Britain weapons, food, and ships anyway, and lets them pay later.
It's called Lend-Lease.
America isn't fighting yet.
But it's definitely picked a side.

## 6. THE BIGGEST INVASION IN HISTORY (1941) [CHAPTER]

*Hitler and Stalin's handshake photo on a wall. It tears in half.*

Remember that deal between Hitler and Stalin?
Hitler never meant to keep it.
On the twenty-second of June 1941, Germany invades the Soviet Union.
It's called Operation Barbarossa, and it's the largest invasion in history.
More than three million soldiers attack along a front line almost three thousand kilometres long. [beat]

*Stalin at his desk, ignoring a pile of letters marked "WARNING". One says "THEY ARE COMING. LOVE, SPY".*

The crazy part? Stalin was warned.
His spies, the British, and even a German soldier who deserted the night before all told him it was coming.
He didn't believe any of them. [beat]

*Huge arrows across a map. Long lines of Soviet prisoners. Burning planes on runways.*

The first weeks are a disaster for the Soviets.
On the first day alone, more than a thousand Soviet planes are destroyed, most of them before they even take off.
Whole armies are surrounded.
In one battle near Kyiv, the Germans capture over six hundred thousand Soviet soldiers. [beat]

*Leningrad in winter. Snow on empty streets. A stick woman pulling a child on a sled. A tiny slice of bread on a plate.*

In the north, the Germans reach Leningrad, the Soviet Union's second-biggest city.
They don't storm it.
They surround it and try to starve it to death. [beat]
At the worst point, people get about one hundred and twenty-five grams of bread a day.
That's roughly two slices, and much of it isn't even flour.
People boil leather belts and scrape the glue off wallpaper to eat it.
In the first winter alone, hundreds of thousands of people starve. [beat]

*A frozen lake at night. A line of trucks driving across the ice with their headlights off.*

The only way in is across a frozen lake.
Trucks drive over the ice at night, bringing in food and taking out children.
The people call it the Road of Life. [beat]

*A concert hall. Thin, exhausted stick musicians in coats. Loudspeakers on rooftops pointing at the German lines.*

And in the summer of 1942, the city does something incredible.
The surviving musicians, so weak that some of them can barely hold their instruments, perform a brand new symphony.
It's broadcast on loudspeakers, pointed straight at the German lines.
The message is simple: we're still here. [beat]
The siege lasts eight hundred and seventy-two days.
Around a million people die.
But Leningrad never falls. [beat]

*Back to the main front. Map arrows toward Moscow.*

Meanwhile, in the center, the main German army pushes on toward Moscow.

*Mud. A German tank sinking up to its turret. Then snow. A German stick soldier in a summer uniform, shivering, icicles on his helmet.*

But then Russia does what Russia does.
First comes the autumn rain, which turns every road into mud.
Then comes the winter.
The Germans planned to win before winter, so most of them don't even have winter coats.
Temperatures drop to minus forty.
Engines freeze. Guns freeze. Soldiers freeze. [beat]

*Soviet ski troops in white, fresh, bursting out of the snow.*

And in December, the Soviets counterattack with fresh troops from Siberia, who are very much dressed for the weather.
The Germans are pushed back from Moscow.
For the first time, Hitler's army has failed at something big. [beat]
And just two days later, on the other side of the world, everything changes again.

## 7. PEARL HARBOR AND THE PACIFIC (1941–1943) [CHAPTER]

*Map: Japan, with a tiny oil gauge on "EMPTY". A US stick official holding a giant tap, turning it off.*

Back in Asia, Japan has been fighting China for four years.
To stop it, America cuts off Japan's oil.
And Japan imports most of its oil from America.
So Japan's leaders face a choice: give up their empire, or take the oil they need by force, from the British and Dutch colonies in Southeast Asia.
They choose force.
But first, they need to stop the American navy from getting in the way. [beat]

*Early morning. Hawaii. Calm harbor, battleships in a neat row. Then the sky fills with planes.*

On the seventh of December 1941, Japanese planes from six aircraft carriers attack the American navy base at Pearl Harbor, in Hawaii.
In under two hours, they sink or damage eight battleships and kill more than two thousand Americans. [beat]
But they miss something important.
America's aircraft carriers aren't in the harbor that day.
And in this new kind of war, carriers are what really matter. [beat]

*Hitler reading the news, then standing up and pointing at America on a globe.*

The next day, America declares war on Japan.
And a few days later, Hitler does something nobody asked him to do.
He declares war on America.
He's now at war with the British Empire, the Soviet Union, and the biggest factory on Earth, all at the same time. [beat]

*Fast montage: Japanese flags appearing across a map of Asia. Hong Kong. The Philippines. Singapore.*

For the next six months, Japan is unstoppable.
Hong Kong, the Philippines, Malaya, and the Dutch East Indies all fall.
The British fortress of Singapore had huge guns pointing out to sea.
The Japanese came from the jungle behind it, on bicycles.
More than eighty thousand British and Commonwealth soldiers surrender.
Churchill calls it the worst disaster in British military history. [beat]

*An aircraft carrier deck. Sixteen big bombers crammed on it, way too big for a carrier. A stick pilot looking at the short deck, gulping.*

America needs some good news.
So in April 1942, it tries something crazy.
Sixteen big army bombers are loaded onto an aircraft carrier, a ship they were never designed to take off from.
And they definitely can't land back on it.
The plan: take off, bomb Tokyo, and keep flying until you reach China. [beat]
It's called the Doolittle Raid, after the pilot who leads it.
The bombs barely scratch Japan, and most of the planes crash-land or run out of fuel over China, where Chinese villagers hide the crews.
Japan takes a terrible revenge on those villages. [beat]
But in Tokyo, Japan's leaders are humiliated.
America just bombed their capital.
So they decide to wipe out what's left of the American navy, once and for all.

*A US stick codebreaker in a basement, surrounded by paper, coffee cups, and a very tired face.*

But in an American basement in Hawaii, codebreakers have been reading parts of Japan's secret messages.
They know Japan is planning a big attack on a target called "AF".
They think AF is Midway, a tiny island in the middle of the Pacific.
So they set a trap.
Midway sends out a fake message saying its water machine is broken.
A few days later, the Japanese send a message saying: AF is short of water. [beat]
Gotcha.

*Ocean. Japanese carriers. American dive bombers falling out of the clouds.*

In June 1942, at the Battle of Midway, the Americans are waiting.
In about five minutes, American dive bombers set three Japanese carriers on fire.
By the end of the battle, Japan has lost four of its biggest carriers.
Japan will never attack in the Pacific on this scale again. [beat]

*Jungle. Rain. A small airstrip. Stick marines in a muddy foxhole, one scratching mosquito bites.*

Two months later, American Marines land on Guadalcanal, a jungle island where Japan is building an airfield.
They grab it in a day.
Keeping it takes six months. [beat]
Every night, Japanese ships race in to drop off troops and shell the airfield.
Every day, American planes chase them away.
And in between, both sides are fighting malaria, rain, and hunger.
Japanese soldiers start calling it Starvation Island. [beat]
In February 1943, Japan quietly pulls out.
From now on, Japan is on defense.

## 8. THE FACTORY WAR [CHAPTER]

*A giant factory. Bombers rolling off a line, one after another, with a big clock above the door.*

Remember when Hitler declared war on the biggest factory on Earth?
Here's why that was a terrible idea. [beat]
American car factories stop making cars and start making tanks, planes, and jeeps.
At its peak, one Ford factory in Michigan builds a four-engine bomber about every hour.
Shipyards build cargo ships so fast that one is put together in under five days.
By the end of the war, America builds around three hundred thousand military planes. [beat]

*A stick woman in a bandana and overalls, flexing. Behind her: a line of women riveting a plane.*

With millions of men away fighting, millions of women take their place in the factories.
They weld, rivet, and build bombers.
They're nicknamed Rosie the Riveter. [beat]

*Soviet factory: a whole factory loaded onto trains, machines and all, heading east past the mountains.*

The Soviets do something even crazier.
When the Germans invade, they take entire factories apart, load them onto trains, and rebuild them on the other side of the Ural Mountains, out of reach of German bombers.
Some start making tanks again within weeks, sometimes before the walls and roofs are even finished. [beat]

*Britain: a stick family at a kitchen table, staring at one egg.*

And in Britain, food is rationed so strictly that a family might get about one egg per person per week.
People dig up their gardens and parks to grow vegetables. [beat]
Germany and Japan have brilliant soldiers and scary weapons.
But the Allies can simply build more of everything.
Battles are won by soldiers.
This war is won by factories.

## 9. THE TURN (1942–1943) [CHAPTER]

*A big wheel or tug-of-war rope sliding the other way. Title text: "1942".*

By late 1942, the Axis controls more of the world than ever before.
And within a few months, it starts falling apart.

*Desert. Rommel's tanks facing a long line of British guns at night. Thousands of muzzle flashes at once.*

In North Africa, the British finally beat Rommel at a place called El Alamein in Egypt.
At the same time, American and British troops land behind him in Morocco and Algeria.
Rommel is squeezed from both sides.
By May 1943, the Axis armies in Africa surrender. [beat]

*Stalingrad: a ruined city, broken buildings, a stick soldier hiding behind a bathtub.*

But the biggest battle is happening in a Soviet city on the Volga river.
Stalingrad.
Hitler wants it because it's an important industrial city, and maybe because it has Stalin's name on it.
Stalin refuses to lose it for exactly the same reason. [beat]
The fighting is street by street, house by house, sometimes room by room.
Soldiers fight over a single staircase.
The city's main train station changes hands more than a dozen times. [beat]

*Map: two huge Soviet arrows curving around the city and meeting behind it. Click.*

Then, in November 1942, the Soviets launch a surprise attack around the city, through the weaker Romanian armies guarding the sides.
In four days, their two arms meet.
The entire German Sixth Army, around three hundred thousand men including their allies, is trapped inside. [beat]
Hitler forbids them to break out.
Instead, he promises to fly in supplies.
The planes bring a fraction of what the army needs.

*A German stick general in a basement, looking at a telegram that says "CONGRATULATIONS: FIELD MARSHAL". Below it: "P.S. NO FIELD MARSHAL HAS EVER SURRENDERED".*

In January 1943, with his men starving, General Paulus gets a promotion to Field Marshal.
It's not a reward.
No German Field Marshal had ever surrendered, so Hitler was basically telling him to fight to the death.
The next day, Paulus surrenders. [beat]
About ninety thousand Germans walk into Soviet captivity.
Only about five thousand of them ever make it home. [beat]

*Ocean. A U-boat periscope. Then a British stick woman at Bletchley Park, at a machine full of wires and rotors.*

Meanwhile, at sea, German submarines called U-boats have been sinking the ships that carry food and weapons to Britain.
But at a country house called Bletchley Park, British codebreakers have cracked the German Enigma code.
Add radar, long-range planes, and better escorts, and in May 1943 alone, the Germans lose forty-one U-boats.
They call it Black May. [beat]

*An endless field of tanks. Two huge stick armies facing each other.*

In the summer of 1943, Germany tries one last big attack in the East, at Kursk.
It turns into one of the largest tank battles in history.
And the Soviets are ready for it with mines, trenches, and guns stretching back for miles.
The attack fails.
From now on, the Soviets do the attacking. [beat]

*Sicily, landing boats. Then Mussolini being led out of a palace by two stick guards.*

That same summer, the Allies land on the Italian island of Sicily.
Within weeks, Mussolini's own party votes against him, and the King of Italy has him arrested.
Italy switches sides.
But German troops pour into Italy and fight for every mountain, so the Allies spend the next two years climbing up the country one hill at a time. [beat]

*Small wink: a foggy island. Stick soldiers carefully approaching. Inside the bunker: a dog.*

Oh, and that same summer, thirty-five thousand American and Canadian soldiers invaded a Japanese-held island near Alaska.
The Japanese had already left.
But that's a story for another video.

## 10. THE HOLOCAUST [CHAPTER: no music sting]

*Tone change. No jokes. Slow camera. Muted colors. Empty shoes, a train track, a closed gate. No violence shown.*

While all of this was happening on the battlefields, something even darker was happening behind the front lines.
From the moment the Nazis took power, they blamed Jewish people for Germany's problems.
First, they took away their jobs and their rights.
Then they forced them into closed-off parts of cities called ghettos. [beat]
When the war began, it got worse.
Special units followed the German army into the Soviet Union and murdered entire Jewish communities.
And in 1942, Nazi leaders met at a villa outside Berlin to plan what they called the "final solution":
the murder of every Jewish person in Europe. [beat]
They built death camps, like Auschwitz, Treblinka, and Sobibor.
People were packed into trains, told they were being moved somewhere new, and killed when they arrived. [beat]
By the end of the war, around six million Jewish people had been murdered.
Millions of others were killed too: Roma, disabled people, Soviet prisoners, Poles, and anyone the Nazis decided didn't belong. [beat]
It's called the Holocaust.
And it's the reason the world still says: never again.

## 11. CLOSING IN (1944) [CHAPTER]

*A German prison camp in a pine forest. Inside a hut, a stick prisoner lifts up a stove. Underneath: a tunnel.*

In March 1944, in a German prison camp for captured airmen, a group of prisoners has been digging.
Not one tunnel. Three.
They name them Tom, Dick, and Harry, so they never have to say the word "tunnel" out loud. [beat]
They hide the sand in little bags inside their trousers and quietly shake it out while walking around the yard.
Harry is over a hundred metres long, with lights, air pumps, and a tiny railway. [beat]

*Night. A hole in the snow, a few metres short of the trees. A guard turns around.*

On a freezing night, seventy-six men crawl through it.
But the tunnel comes up a few metres short of the forest, and a guard spots them.
Almost all of them are caught.
Hitler is furious, and fifty of them are shot. [beat]
Only three make it all the way home.
It becomes known as the Great Escape. [beat]

*An inflatable tank being blown up with a bicycle pump. A stick general standing proudly next to it: Patton.*

That same spring, the Allies are finally ready to invade France.
The Germans know it's coming.
They just don't know where. [beat]
So the Allies build a fake army.
Inflatable tanks, fake landing boats, fake radio traffic, and a real, famous general, George Patton, put in charge of it.
All of it pointing at the narrowest part of the Channel, near Calais.
The Germans fall for it completely. [beat]

*Dawn. Normandy. Huge fleet. A German bunker; a stick officer on the phone, nobody answering.*

On the sixth of June 1944, D-Day, more than one hundred and fifty thousand Allied soldiers land on five beaches in Normandy.
Rommel, now in charge of defending the coast, is at home in Germany.
It's his wife's birthday.
And Hitler is asleep, and nobody wants to be the one to wake him up. [beat]

*Omaha beach: stick soldiers huddled behind metal obstacles. Then slowly moving forward.*

On one beach, codenamed Omaha, things go horribly wrong.
The bombs miss, the tanks sink, and the soldiers are pinned down by machine guns.
But slowly, in small groups, they push up the cliffs.
By the end of the day, the Allies are in France to stay. [beat]

*Eastern front map. A massive Soviet arrow smashes through the center.*

And while everyone remembers D-Day, the Soviets launch something even bigger a few weeks later.
Operation Bagration.
In just a few weeks, they destroy almost an entire German army group and push hundreds of kilometres west.
It's one of the worst defeats in German history, and most people in the West have never even heard of it. [beat]

*A meeting room. A briefcase under a heavy wooden table. A stick officer nudges it with his foot to the other side of a thick table leg.*

Inside Germany, some officers realise the war is lost.
So in July 1944, a colonel named Claus von Stauffenberg walks into a meeting with Hitler carrying a briefcase with a bomb inside.
He leaves it under the table and walks out.
The bomb goes off.
But someone had moved the briefcase behind a thick wooden table leg.
Hitler survives with burst eardrums and torn trousers.
Stauffenberg is executed that night. [beat]

*Warsaw. Polish resistance fighters with red-and-white armbands on rooftops. Across a river, Soviet tanks just... parked.*

In August 1944, the Soviet army reaches the edge of Warsaw.
Believing help is coming, the Polish resistance rises up against the Germans.
But the Soviet army stops on the other side of the river, and waits.
Stalin doesn't want a free, independent Poland after the war, and many historians believe he was happy to let the Germans destroy the people who would lead it. [beat]
The Poles fight alone for sixty-three days.
When it's over, up to two hundred thousand people are dead, and Hitler orders what's left of the city blown up, street by street. [beat]

*Paris: cheering crowds, flowers. Then a long bridge in Holland, too far away.*

That same month, Paris rises up too, and this time help arrives.
Paris is liberated.
In September, the Allies try to end the war by Christmas with a giant parachute drop into Holland, to grab a chain of bridges.
They grab most of them. But not the last one, at Arnhem.
It was, quite literally, a bridge too far. [beat]

*Snowy forest, December. German tanks bursting out of the fog. A surrounded town: Bastogne.*

And in December 1944, Hitler throws his last reserves into a surprise attack through, you guessed it, the Ardennes forest again.
It's the Battle of the Bulge.
In the town of Bastogne, surrounded American troops are told to surrender.
Their general sends back a reply with one word.
"Nuts." [beat]
The Germans had no idea what it meant.
But they figured out it wasn't a yes.

## 12. THE END IN EUROPE (1945) [CHAPTER]

*Map: Allied arrows from the west, Soviet arrows from the east, squeezing Germany like a sandwich.*

By 1945, Germany is being crushed from both sides.
Allied bombers have flattened its cities.
And Germany is now sending old men and teenage boys to fight. [beat]

*Three stick leaders at a table by the sea: Churchill, Roosevelt, Stalin. Cutting a cake shaped like Germany.*

In February, Churchill, Roosevelt, and Stalin meet at Yalta to decide what Europe will look like after the war.
Germany will be split into zones.
And Stalin promises free elections in Eastern Europe. [beat]
He does not keep that promise.

*A newspaper headline. A flag at half-mast.*

In April, Roosevelt dies, just weeks before the victory he worked so hard for.
Mussolini is caught by Italian partisans while trying to escape to Switzerland, and executed.

*Berlin. Ruins. Soviet tanks in the streets. A bunker door closes.*

The Soviets reach Berlin.
And on the thirtieth of April 1945, deep in a bunker under the city, Adolf Hitler takes his own life. [beat]
A week later, Germany surrenders.
On the eighth of May, people pour into the streets in London, Paris, and New York to celebrate Victory in Europe.
The war in Europe is over.
But on the other side of the world, it's still going.

## 13. THE END IN THE PACIFIC (1944–1945) [CHAPTER]

*Pacific map. A dotted line of islands. A stick American hopping from one to the next, like a game of hopscotch.*

Back in the Pacific, ever since Guadalcanal, the Americans have been fighting their way toward Japan, one island at a time.
They skip the strongest islands and grab the ones they need for airfields, letting the rest starve.
They call it island hopping. [beat]
And every island is bloodier than the last. [beat]

*Ocean, seen from above: hundreds of tiny ships. Japanese carriers far to the north, with almost no planes on their decks.*

In October 1944, the Americans return to the Philippines, and Japan throws almost its entire navy at them.
The Battle of Leyte Gulf is, by many measures, the biggest naval battle in history.
Japan's plan is wild.
Its once-mighty aircraft carriers, now with barely any planes or pilots left, sail out as bait, to lure the main American fleet away.
And it actually works. [beat]
But it doesn't matter.
By the end of the battle, Japan's navy is basically gone. [beat]

*A single plane diving at a ship. Slow, serious.*

It's also where Japan tries something new and terrible.
Pilots are ordered to crash their planes straight into American ships, on purpose.
They're called kamikazes.
By the end of the war, thousands of young pilots die this way. [beat]
In early 1945, on Iwo Jima, a tiny island covered in volcanic sand, almost every Japanese defender fights to the death.
On Okinawa, the battle lasts nearly three months. [beat]

*Night bombers over a city of wooden houses. Orange glow.*

American bombers burn Japanese cities.
In one night in March 1945, a firebombing raid on Tokyo kills around one hundred thousand people.
But Japan still refuses to surrender.
And the Americans are planning an invasion of Japan itself that they expect to cost hundreds of thousands of lives on both sides. [beat]

*Slow. Quiet. A desert in New Mexico. A flash. Then a single bomber flying over a city in the morning. No impact shown: just the light, and silence.*

Then, in July 1945, in the New Mexico desert, scientists test a new kind of weapon.
An atomic bomb. [beat]
On the sixth of August 1945, an American bomber drops one on the city of Hiroshima.
One bomb destroys most of the city.
Three days later, a second bomb hits Nagasaki.
Together, they kill more than one hundred thousand people, many of them in an instant. [beat]
In between the two bombs, the Soviet Union declares war on Japan and smashes into Manchuria. [beat]

*A radio in a Japanese home. Families kneeling around it, listening.*

On the fifteenth of August, the Japanese people hear something they have never heard before.
The voice of their Emperor, on the radio.
He tells them that Japan will "endure the unendurable" and surrender.
On the second of September 1945, Japan signs the surrender on board the battleship USS Missouri, in Tokyo Bay. [beat]
After six years and one day, World War Two is over.

## 14. AFTER THE WAR [CHAPTER]

*A world map, cities in ruins slowly being rebuilt. Two giant stick figures, America and the Soviet Union, eyeing each other across Europe.*

More than seventy million people are dead.
That's about one in every thirty people alive on Earth back then. [beat]
Nazi leaders are put on trial at Nuremberg for their crimes.
Fifty countries create the United Nations, a new and hopefully better group chat, so that this never happens again.
Germany is split in two.
And the two strongest countries left standing, America and the Soviet Union, immediately start eyeing each other.
That's the beginning of the Cold War. [beat]

*Jungle. Same soldier from the cold open. Older, beard, uniform patched, still holding his rifle.*

Oh, and remember that soldier from the beginning?
His name was Hiroo Onoda, a Japanese officer sent to a jungle island in the Philippines in 1944, with orders never to surrender.
Nobody told him the war was over.
Or rather, people tried. They dropped leaflets. He decided they were a trick. [beat]
So he kept fighting in the jungle for twenty-nine more years.
In 1974, a young Japanese traveller finally found him.
And Onoda still refused to give up, until his old commanding officer flew all the way out to the island and officially ordered him to stop. [beat]
He handed over his sword, his rifle, and his ammunition, still in working order.
World War Two was finally, completely, over.

*Onoda saluting. Cut to black.*

## FACT SHEET (sources)
- Versailles 1919: army capped at 100,000, war-guilt clause, reparations. Germany made its final WWI-related debt payment on 3 Oct 2010 (BBC, 2010).
- Hyperinflation: a loaf of bread cost about 200 billion marks in November 1923. Workers were paid twice daily; children played with banknote bundles (Britannica; Deutsches Historisches Museum).
- Depression: about 6 million unemployed in Germany by early 1932 (Britannica).
- Hitler: rejected by the Vienna Academy of Fine Arts in 1907 and 1908. Joined the DAP (later NSDAP) in 1919. Chancellor on 30 Jan 1933 (Britannica; USHMM).
- Mukden Incident, 18 Sep 1931: officers of Japan's Kwantung Army set off a small explosion on the railway. Japan left the League of Nations in 1933 after the Lytton Report (Britannica).
- Italy invaded Ethiopia in Oct 1935; League sanctions were ineffective (Britannica).
- Rhineland, 7 Mar 1936: German troops had contingency orders to withdraw if France resisted (Shirer; Britannica).
- Anschluss Mar 1938. Munich Agreement 30 Sep 1938, "peace for our time". Rest of Czechoslovakia occupied 15 Mar 1939 (IWM).
- Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact 23 Aug 1939, with a secret protocol dividing Poland (Britannica). Hitler's Mein Kampf calls for Lebensraum in the East.
- Germany invaded Poland 1 Sep; Britain and France declared war 3 Sep; USSR invaded 17 Sep. Last Polish forces surrendered 6 Oct 1939 (about 5 weeks) (IWM; Britannica).
- Krojanty, 1 Sep 1939: Polish lancers charged German infantry, then were hit by armoured cars. The tank-charge story came from propaganda and war correspondents (Britannica; Polish sources).
- Phoney War: British "Nickel" leaflet raids; "Bore War" nickname (IWM; RAF Museum).
- Winter War Nov 1939–Mar 1940: Finnish ski troops, heavy Soviet losses (Britannica).
- Denmark, 9 Apr 1940: surrendered within about 6 hours. Norway campaign Apr–Jun 1940 (IWM).
- Fall Gelb 10 May 1940 through the Ardennes; France had more tanks (Frieser, The Blitzkrieg Legend; IWM).
- Dunkirk 26 May–4 Jun 1940: about 338,000 evacuated, including "little ships"; Halt Order 24 May (IWM).
- French armistice 22 Jun 1940 at Compiègne, in the same railway carriage as 1918, taken from its museum (Britannica).
- Battle of Britain Jul–Oct 1940: Chain Home radar. Operation Sea Lion postponed in Sep 1940 (IWM; RAF Museum).
- Blitz Sep 1940–May 1941 (about 8 months); Underground stations used as shelters (IWM).
- Operation Compass Dec 1940–Feb 1941: about 130,000 Italian prisoners (IWM). Italy invaded Greece in Oct 1940 and was pushed back (Britannica).
- Rommel sent to Libya in Feb 1941; "Desert Fox" (IWM).
- Lend-Lease Act Mar 1941 (US National Archives).
- Soviet air losses on 22 Jun 1941: about 1,200 aircraft, most on the ground (Glantz).
- Barbarossa 22 Jun 1941: about 3 million+ German troops (3.8M with allies), front about 2,900 km (Britannica; Glantz). Warnings from Sorge, the British, and German deserter Alfred Liskow on the night of 21 Jun (Glantz).
- Kyiv encirclement Sep 1941: about 600,000+ prisoners (Glantz).
- Soviet counteroffensive at Moscow 5 Dec 1941, including Siberian and Far East divisions (Glantz; Britannica).
- US oil embargo Jul/Aug 1941; Japan imported about 80% of its oil from the US (Britannica; Naval History and Heritage Command).
- Pearl Harbor 7 Dec 1941: 6 carriers, 8 battleships sunk or damaged, 2,403 Americans killed, US carriers absent (NHHC; National WWII Museum).
- Germany declared war on the US 11 Dec 1941 (Britannica).
- Singapore fell 15 Feb 1942: about 80,000 British, Indian and Australian troops captured; Japanese troops used bicycles in Malaya; Churchill called it the "worst disaster" (IWM; Churchill, The Hinge of Fate).
- Midway: Station HYPO; the "AF is short of water" ruse (NHHC). 4 Jun 1942: three carriers hit in about 5–6 minutes; Japan lost 4 carriers (NHHC).
- El Alamein Oct–Nov 1942; Operation Torch 8 Nov 1942; Axis surrender in Tunisia 13 May 1943 (IWM).
- Stalingrad: Operation Uranus 19 Nov 1942, encirclement closed 23 Nov at Kalach (4 days), through Romanian sectors; about 250–300,000 trapped (Glantz; Beevor). Paulus promoted 30 Jan 1943 and surrendered 31 Jan. About 91,000 captured, about 5–6,000 returned (Beevor). Stalingrad main railway station changed hands about 13–15 times (Beevor).
- Black May 1943: 41 U-boats lost; Bletchley Park Enigma (Uboat.net; IWM).
- Kursk Jul 1943, including Prokhorovka; defence in depth (Glantz & House).
- Sicily 10 Jul 1943; Mussolini deposed and arrested 25 Jul; Italian armistice announced 8 Sep 1943 (IWM).
- Kiska: 15 Aug 1943, about 35,000 US/Canadian troops, the island already evacuated (see Kiska Short SCRIPT.md).
- Holocaust: Einsatzgruppen; Wannsee Conference 20 Jan 1942; Auschwitz, Treblinka, Sobibor; about 6 million Jews murdered; Roma, disabled people, Soviet POWs, Poles and others (USHMM).
- Fortitude South: First US Army Group under Patton, inflatable decoys, Pas-de-Calais (IWM).
- D-Day 6 Jun 1944: about 156,000 landed; Rommel at home for his wife's birthday; Hitler asleep, staff reluctant to wake him (IWM; National WWII Museum). Omaha: bombs off target, DD tanks swamped (NHHC).
- Bagration 22 Jun 1944: destruction of Army Group Centre, about 28 divisions destroyed, called Germany's worst defeat of the war (Glantz; Zaloga).
- 20 July plot 1944: briefcase moved behind the heavy table support; Hitler suffered burst eardrums and torn trousers; Stauffenberg shot that night (IWM; German Resistance Memorial Center).
- Paris liberated 25 Aug 1944. Market Garden Sep 1944, Arnhem bridge, "A Bridge Too Far" (IWM).
- Bulge 16 Dec 1944; McAuliffe's "Nuts!" on 22 Dec 1944 (US Army Center of Military History).
- Yalta Feb 1945: zones, a promise of free elections in Poland (Britannica).
- FDR died 12 Apr 1945; Mussolini executed 28 Apr; Hitler's suicide 30 Apr; surrender 7–8 May, VE Day 8 May (IWM).
- Iwo Jima Feb–Mar 1945: about 21,000 defenders, about 216 taken prisoner. Okinawa Apr–Jun 1945 (about 82 days) (NHHC).
- Kamikaze: about 3,800 pilots killed (National WWII Museum).
- Tokyo firebombing 9–10 Mar 1945: about 100,000 killed (National WWII Museum).
- Trinity 16 Jul 1945. Hiroshima 6 Aug, Nagasaki 9 Aug; combined deaths over 100,000 in 1945 (estimates up to about 210,000) (Atomic Heritage Foundation). USSR declared war 8 Aug and invaded Manchuria 9 Aug.
- Jewel Voice Broadcast 15 Aug 1945, "enduring the unendurable". Surrender aboard USS Missouri 2 Sep 1945; Germany invaded Poland 1 Sep 1939, so 6 years and 1 day (NHHC).
- Deaths: about 70–85 million, about 3% of the 1940 population (about 2.3 billion), roughly 1 in 30 (National WWII Museum).
- UN Charter signed by 50 countries in Jun 1945 (Poland signed later as the 51st founding member) (UN). Nuremberg trials from Nov 1945.
- Hiroo Onoda: Lubang Island, sent Dec 1944, leaflets dismissed as a trick; found by Norio Suzuki in Feb 1974; relieved by his former commander Maj. Yoshimi Taniguchi on 9 Mar 1974; surrendered his sword and rifle in working order (Britannica; NYT obituary 2014). 1944–1974 = 29 years from the war's end.

- China: Japanese leaders expected a quick war; War Minister Sugiyama assured Hirohito it would end within months (Bix, Hirohito). Nanjing Dec 1937–Jan 1938, about 6 weeks; death estimates range from tens of thousands to over 200,000 (Britannica; IMTFE). About 1 million+ Japanese troops in China for most of the war (Mitter, Forgotten Ally).
- Leningrad: encircled 8 Sep 1941, lifted 27 Jan 1944, 872 days. Lowest ration 125 g/day for dependants and children (Nov–Dec 1941), bread with filler (sawdust/cellulose). Wallpaper paste and leather eaten. Road of Life over Lake Ladoga. Shostakovich's 7th performed in the city 9 Aug 1942 and broadcast by loudspeaker toward German lines. About 800,000–1,000,000+ civilian deaths (Britannica; Reid, Leningrad).
- Doolittle Raid 18 Apr 1942: 16 B-25s from USS Hornet, could not land back aboard, flew on to China; Chinese civilians sheltered crews; Japanese reprisals (Zhejiang–Jiangxi campaign). The raid hardened Japan's decision to attack Midway (NHHC; National WWII Museum).
- Guadalcanal 7 Aug 1942–Feb 1943: airfield (Henderson Field) taken on day 2 and held 6 months; nightly "Tokyo Express" runs; malaria; Japanese troops called it "Starvation Island" (Ga-tō); evacuation Feb 1943 (NHHC).
- Factory war: Ford Willow Run built a B-24 about every 63 minutes at peak (The Henry Ford). Liberty ship SS Robert E. Peary built in 4 days 15 h (Nov 1942) (US Maritime Administration). US built about 300,000 aircraft 1940–45 (National WWII Museum). About 6 million women entered the US workforce, "Rosie the Riveter". USSR evacuated about 1,500 factories east in 1941; some resumed production within weeks, before buildings were finished (Harrison; Britannica). UK rationing: about 1 shell egg per person per week (IWM).
- Great Escape, Stalag Luft III, night of 24–25 Mar 1944: tunnels Tom, Dick, Harry; Harry about 102 m with lights, air pump and trolley rail; sand dispersed via trouser bags ("penguins"); 76 escaped, the exit fell short of the tree line; 73 recaptured, 50 executed on Hitler's orders, 3 reached home (2 Norwegians, 1 Dutchman) (IWM; RAF Museum).
- Warsaw Uprising 1 Aug–2 Oct 1944, 63 days; Red Army halted across the Vistula; Stalin refused landing rights to Allied supply flights for weeks; about 150,000–200,000 civilians killed; Hitler ordered the city razed (Britannica; Warsaw Rising Museum).
- Leyte Gulf 23–26 Oct 1944, often called the largest naval battle in history; Ozawa's near-empty carriers as decoys lured Halsey north; first organized kamikaze attacks on 25 Oct 1944; Japanese navy crippled (NHHC).
